# Western Philosophy: A Lineage Map
*A 10,000-foot genealogy. Arrows (→) mark direct influence or reaction. Each name: 3–6 words on the core contribution.*

---

## I. PRE-SOCRATICS (c. 600–400 BCE)
*The question: what is the world made of, and does it change?*

- **Milesians (Ionia)** — first naturalistic explanations
  - Thales — water as first principle
  - Anaximander — the boundless (apeiron)
  - Anaximenes — air, condensation/rarefaction
- **Pythagoras** — reality is number; soul transmigration
- **Heraclitus** — flux, fire, logos; "everything flows"
- **Eleatics** (anti-Heraclitus)
  - Parmenides — being is one, unchanging; reason over senses
  - Zeno — paradoxes defending Parmenides
- **Pluralists** (reconciling change with being)
  - Empedocles — four elements, love/strife
  - Anaxagoras — mind (nous) orders cosmos
- **Atomists**
  - Democritus — atoms and void; materialism
- **Sophists** — rhetoric, relativism, "man the measure"
  - Protagoras, Gorgias

---

## II. CLASSICAL ATHENS (c. 470–322 BCE)
*The turn to ethics, knowledge, and the ideal state*

- **Socrates** — the examined life; elenchus (questioning method); ethics as knowledge
  - → **Plato** — theory of Forms; ideal state; soul's immortality; wrote the dialogues
    - → **Aristotle** — logic; empiricism; virtue ethics; four causes; systematized every field
      - *(His works dominate the next 1,800 years)*

---

## III. HELLENISTIC & ROMAN (c. 322 BCE – 500 CE)
*Philosophy as a way of life: how to be tranquil*

- **Cynics** — Diogenes: reject convention, live naturally
- **Stoics** — virtue alone suffices; accept fate; cosmic reason
  - Zeno of Citium (founder) → Epictetus, Seneca, Marcus Aurelius
- **Epicureans** — Epicurus: pleasure = absence of pain; atomism; don't fear death
- **Skeptics**
  - Pyrrhonian — Pyrrho, Sextus Empiricus: suspend judgment; peace through doubt
  - Academic — Arcesilaus, Carneades: Plato's own Academy turns skeptical
- **Cicero** — translated Greek thought into Latin; *(the Renaissance humanists' source text; → Montaigne)*
- **Neoplatonism** — Plotinus: the One, emanation; mystical Platonism
  - *(Bridge to Christian thought)*

---

## IV. MEDIEVAL (c. 400–1400)
*Faith seeking understanding: reconciling Greek reason with revelation*

- **Augustine** (354–430) — Platonized Christianity; time, will, original sin
- **Boethius** — transmitted logic to Latin West; consolation of philosophy
- **Pseudo-Dionysius** — apophatic (negative) theology; Christian Neoplatonism *(→ Aquinas, Eckhart, mystical line)*
- *(Transmission via Islamic & Jewish philosophers: Avicenna — essence/existence; Averroes — Aristotle commentaries; Maimonides — negative theology. They preserved and returned Aristotle to Europe.)*
- **Anselm** — ontological argument for God
- **Abelard** — logic, ethics of intention
- **Aquinas** (1225–1274) — Aristotle + Christianity synthesis; five ways; natural law
- **Duns Scotus** — univocity of being; haecceity (thisness)
- **William of Ockham** — nominalism; Ockham's razor; *(begins unraveling scholasticism)*

---

## V. RENAISSANCE (c. 1400–1600)
*Recovery of the ancients; turn to the human*

- **Humanists** — Petrarch, Erasmus, Pico: human dignity, classical revival
- **Machiavelli** — realist politics; power divorced from morality
- **Montaigne** — the essay; revived skepticism; self-examination
- **Francis Bacon** — inductive method; science as power over nature

---

## VI. EARLY MODERN (c. 1600–1780)
*The epistemological turn: what can I know, and how?*

### Rationalists *(knowledge from reason)*
- **Descartes** (1596–1650) — cogito; mind-body dualism; methodic doubt; *(father of modern philosophy)*
  - → **Spinoza** — one substance (God = Nature); determinism; geometric ethics
  - → **Leibniz** — monads; best possible world; calculus; sufficient reason
- **Pascal** *(counter-voice to Descartes)* — limits of reason; the Wager; "the heart has its reasons" *(→ Kierkegaard, existentialism)*

### Empiricists *(knowledge from experience)*
- **Locke** — mind as blank slate; primary/secondary qualities
  - → **Berkeley** — idealism; "to be is to be perceived"
    - → **Hume** — causation is habit; is/ought gap; radical skepticism
      - *(Wakes Kant from "dogmatic slumber")*

### Political philosophy *(parallel track)*
- **Hobbes** — social contract; state of nature is war; Leviathan
- **Locke** — natural rights; government by consent *(→ American founding)*
- **Rousseau** — general will; noble savage; inequality critique *(→ French Revolution)*
- **Montesquieu** — separation of powers

### Counter-Enlightenment seeds *(history and culture against universal reason)*
- **Vico** — *verum factum*: we know only what we make; philosophy of history *(→ Hegel, hermeneutics — largely retrospective influence)*
- **Herder** — cultures as organic wholes; language shapes thought *(→ Romanticism, historicism, nationalism)*

---

## VII. KANT: THE GREAT SYNTHESIS (1724–1804)
*Merges rationalism and empiricism*

- **Kant** — mind structures experience (Copernican revolution); noumena/phenomena; categorical imperative; *(nearly all later philosophy responds to him)*

---

## VIII. POST-KANT: THE 19TH CENTURY
*Splinters in every direction*

### German Idealism *(radicalizing Kant)*
- **Fichte** — the self posits reality
- **Schelling** — nature and spirit identical
- **Hegel** (1770–1831) — dialectic; history as Spirit's self-realization; master-slave
  - → **Feuerbach** — God is humanity's self-projection; idealism inverted
    - → **Marx** — materialist dialectic; class struggle; "philosophers interpreted, point is to change"
  - → **British Idealists** (Bradley, McTaggart) — *(later demolished by Russell/Moore)*

### Reactions against Hegel/system-building
- **Schopenhauer** — blind Will drives all; pessimism; art as escape *(imports Upanishads)*
- **Kierkegaard** — subjective truth; leap of faith; *(father of existentialism)*
- **Nietzsche** — God is dead; will to power; genealogy of morals; eternal recurrence

### Hermeneutic bridge
- **Dilthey** — human sciences (Verstehen) vs natural sciences (Erklären) *(→ Heidegger, Gadamer)*

### Utilitarians *(British ethical line)*
- **Bentham** — greatest happiness calculus
- **J.S. Mill** — refined utilitarianism; liberty; harm principle

### American Pragmatism
- **Peirce** — meaning = practical consequences; semiotics
- **James** — truth is what works; radical empiricism
- **Dewey** — instrumentalism; education, democracy

---

## IX. THE 20TH-CENTURY SPLIT
*Two traditions that barely speak to each other*

### A. ANALYTIC (Anglo-American: logic, language, clarity)
- **Frege** — modern logic; sense vs reference *(founding father)*
  - → **Russell** — logical atomism; theory of descriptions; Principia
  - → **Moore** — common sense; naturalistic fallacy
  - → **Wittgenstein I** (Tractatus) — language pictures world; "whereof one cannot speak..."
    - → **Vienna Circle / Logical Positivism** — Carnap, Ayer: verification principle; metaphysics is nonsense
      - → **Popper** — falsification, not verification *(critic from outside)*
      - → **Quine** — demolished analytic/synthetic distinction; holism
  - → **Wittgenstein II** (Investigations) — meaning is use; language games; private language impossible
    - → **Ordinary language school** — Ryle (category mistakes, ghost in machine), Austin (speech acts)
- **Later analytic branches:**
  - Anscombe — intention, action theory; fired the starting gun for virtue ethics revival *(1958, before MacIntyre)*
  - Sellars — demolished the "Myth of the Given" *(→ Rorty, Brandom, McDowell)*
  - Rorty — anti-foundationalism; pragmatism revived; bridged analytic to continental/postmodern
  - Kripke — necessity, rigid designators, naming
  - Davidson — meaning, action, anomalous monism
  - Putnam — twin earth; meaning ain't in the head
  - **Rawls** — justice as fairness; veil of ignorance *(revived political philosophy)*
    - → Nozick — libertarian rejoinder; minimal state
  - Parfit — personal identity doesn't matter; ethics
  - **Philosophy of mind:** Dennett (consciousness deflated), Chalmers (hard problem), Searle (Chinese room), Nagel (what is it like to be a bat)

### B. CONTINENTAL (European: existence, history, power)
- **Brentano** — intentionality: consciousness is always *about* something *(Husserl's teacher; concept also crossed to analytic mind)*
- **Bergson** — lived time (durée); intuition over intellect; vitalism *(→ Deleuze; phenomenology's rival and cousin)*
- **Husserl** — phenomenology; bracket assumptions, describe experience
  - → **Heidegger** — Being and time; Dasein; being-toward-death; *(also → hermeneutics)*
    - → **Sartre** — existence precedes essence; radical freedom; bad faith
    - → **Merleau-Ponty** — embodied perception
    - → **Gadamer** — hermeneutics; fusion of horizons
    - → **Levinas** — ethics of the Other's face
  - → **Beauvoir** — one is not born a woman; existentialist feminism
- **Frankfurt School** *(Marx + Freud)*
  - Horkheimer & Adorno — dialectic of enlightenment; culture industry
  - Benjamin — art in mechanical reproduction; messianic history *(outsider member; huge in media/cultural theory)*
  - Marcuse — one-dimensional man
  - → **Habermas** — communicative rationality; public sphere
- **Structuralism** — Saussure (linguistics), Lévi-Strauss (anthropology), Lacan (unconscious structured like language; *→ Žižek, Butler, film/cultural theory*)
  - → **Post-structuralism**
    - **Foucault** — power/knowledge; discipline; genealogy of institutions
    - **Derrida** — deconstruction; différance; no fixed meaning
    - **Deleuze** — difference, rhizomes, desire
- **Others:** Arendt (banality of evil, action), Camus (absurd, revolt), Ricoeur (narrative identity)

---

## X. LATE 20TH – 21ST CENTURY
*Fragmentation, applied turn, and new problems*

- **Ethics revived:**
  - MacIntyre — return to virtue ethics
  - Williams — moral luck, against system
  - Singer — animal liberation, effective altruism
  - Korsgaard — Kantian constructivism
  - Nussbaum — capabilities approach
- **Analytic metaphysics resurgent:** Lewis (possible worlds realism), Williamson (knowledge first)
- **Philosophy of mind/AI:** Chalmers (consciousness, simulation), Clark (extended mind), Bostrom (superintelligence, existential risk), Floridi (philosophy of information)
- **Continental continuations:** Žižek (Lacan + Hegel + pop culture), Agamben (state of exception), Butler (gender performativity), Meillassoux (speculative realism — against Kant's correlationism)
- **Experimental philosophy** — testing intuitions empirically
- **Emerging fronts:** philosophy of AI and machine ethics; digital personhood; climate/intergenerational ethics

---

## THE SPINE (if you remember nothing else)

```
Pre-Socratics (nature)
   → Socrates/Plato/Aristotle (ethics, forms, logic)
      → Hellenistic schools (how to live)
         → Medieval synthesis (reason + faith: Aquinas)
            → Descartes (epistemological turn)
               → Rationalists vs Empiricists
                  → KANT (synthesis)
                     → Hegel (history) → Marx / Kierkegaard / Nietzsche (revolt)
                        → 20th c. SPLIT:
                           Analytic (Frege→Russell→Wittgenstein→Quine→Kripke/Rawls)
                           Continental (Husserl→Heidegger→Sartre/Foucault/Derrida)
                              → 21st c.: mind, AI, ethics, fragmentation
```

**Four recurring pivots:**
1. **Socrates** — from cosmos to ethics
2. **Descartes** — from world to knower
3. **Kant** — from knower to conditions of knowing
4. **Frege/Husserl** — from consciousness to language/experience (the split)
