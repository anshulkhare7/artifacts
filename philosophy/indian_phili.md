# Indian Thought: A Lineage Map
*A 10,000-foot genealogy of Indian philosophy — darshana, dharma, and their neighbors. Arrows (→) mark direct influence or reaction. A caution unique to this lineage: authorship is often attribution, dates are contested by centuries, and schools speak through anonymous sutras and layered commentaries. Where the person is unverifiable, the **text or the idea** carries the line.*

---

## I. THE VEDIC AGE (c. 1500–800 BCE)
*Ritual, cosmic order — and the first recorded doubt*

- **Rigveda** — 1,028 hymns; *rita* (cosmic order) stands even above the gods
  - **Nasadiya Sukta** (X.129) — creation hymn that ends in skepticism: "Whence this creation has arisen — perhaps it formed itself, perhaps not — he who surveys it from highest heaven, only he knows. *Or perhaps he does not know.*" *(doubt is present at the very source)*
  - **Purusha Sukta** — cosmos from the dismemberment of a primal being; *(the charter myth of varna — contested ever after; → Manusmriti; Ambedkar's target)*
- **Brahmanas** — sacrifice as cosmic technology; ritual correctly performed sustains the world
- **Aranyakas** — "forest texts"; the ritual internalized, symbolized, meditated upon *(bridge to the Upanishads)*

---

## II. THE UPANISHADS (c. 800–300 BCE)
*The turn inward: from outer sacrifice to the knowing self*

- **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad** (oldest) — the great dialogues at Janaka's court
  - **Yajnavalkya** — *neti neti* ("not this, not this"); the self as the unknowable knower; the earliest recorded karma-rebirth teaching *(arguably the first named philosopher anywhere)*
  - **Gargi Vachaknavi** — the woman who questions him past the edge of the sayable: "On what is the world woven, warp and weft?"
  - **Maitreyi** — chooses the knowledge of immortality over wealth
- **Chandogya Upanishad**
  - **Uddalaka Aruni** — *tat tvam asi* ("that thou art"); teaches by salt dissolved in water; being (*sat*) as the root of all *(→ every Vedanta)*
- **Katha Upanishad** — Nachiketa bargains with Death; the chariot analogy of the self *(→ Gita)*
- **Mandukya Upanishad** — AUM; waking, dream, deep sleep, and *turiya*, the fourth *(→ Gaudapada, Advaita)*
- **Shvetashvatara Upanishad** — the theistic turn; Brahman as a personal Lord *(→ Gita, Shaivism, bhakti)*
- **The frame crystallizes:** Brahman = Atman; karma; samsara; moksha
  - *(every later school — orthodox or heretic — orients to this frame, for or against)*

---

## III. THE SHRAMANA REVOLT (c. 600–300 BCE)
*Renouncers against the Veda; materialists against everything*

> **THE GREAT FORK.** Schools split over the Veda's authority: **āstika** (accept it — the six darshanas) vs **nāstika** (reject it — Buddhists, Jains, Ajivikas, Charvakas). The fork organizes everything after.

- **The Buddha** (Siddhartha Gautama, c. 5th c. BCE) — four noble truths; the middle way; *anatta* (no-self); dependent origination *(accepts karma, rejects the atman — the Upanishads' great rival)*
- **Mahavira** (trad. 599–527 BCE) — 24th tirthankara of Jainism; *ahimsa* absolute; *anekantavada* (many-sidedness of truth); karma as subtle matter
- **The lost schools** *(known only through their enemies' quotations)*
  - **Makkhali Gosala** (Ajivika) — *niyati*: iron determinism; liberation arrives on schedule, effort changes nothing
  - **Ajita Kesakambali** — earliest materialist on record; no afterlife, no karma, no debt to priests
  - **Sanjaya Belatthiputta** — suspension of judgment on every metaphysical question *(compare Pyrrho — who traveled to India with Alexander and returned a skeptic)*
  - **Charvaka / Lokayata** (attr. Brihaspati) — perception the only valid knowledge; consciousness from matter as intoxication from grain; "while life is yours, live joyously"
- **The science of language** *(the astika side's secret weapon)*
  - **Panini** (c. 4th c. BCE) — the *Ashtadhyayi*: ~4,000 rules generating all of Sanskrit; generative grammar 2,400 years before Chomsky *(the model of systematic thought for every darshana — India's Euclid)*

---

## IV. EPIC & EMPIRE (c. 400 BCE – 400 CE)
*Dharma dramatized, codified, and politicized*

- **Mahabharata** — ethics through catastrophe; "dharma is subtle" — every principle fails somewhere
  - **Bhagavad Gita** — the synthesis: act without attachment (*nishkama karma*); knowledge, action, and devotion as three yogas in one poem *(answers the renouncers without leaving the world; → Jnaneshwar, Gandhi, Aurobindo)*
- **Kautilya** (attr., c. 300 BCE; text layered later) — *Arthashastra*: statecraft without illusions; the mandala of allies and enemies *(India's Machiavelli, ~1,800 years earlier)*
- **Manusmriti** (c. 200 BCE–200 CE) — dharma codified into law and varna *(the most contested text in the lineage; Ambedkar burned it, 1927)*
- **Ashoka** (r. 268–232 BCE) — Buddhist *dhamma* as imperial policy; edicts in stone on tolerance, welfare, remorse *(→ Amartya Sen's public reason)*

---

## V. THE SIX DARSHANAS (c. 200 BCE – 500 CE)
*Orthodox worldviews compressed into sutras — each school a founding aphorism-text plus centuries of commentary*

*(The six come in traditional pairs:)*

- **Samkhya** (attr. Kapila; systematized in Ishvarakrishna's *Samkhyakarika*, c. 350 CE) — radical dualism: *purusha* (consciousness) vs *prakriti* (nature); 25 tattvas; liberation by discrimination; godless *(the background metaphysics of Yoga, the Gita, and Ayurveda)*
- **Yoga** (Patanjali's *Yoga Sutras*, c. 200–400 CE) — "yoga is the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind"; the eight limbs; Samkhya made practice
- **Nyaya** (attr. Gautama Akshapada) — logic and epistemology: four pramanas (perception, inference, comparison, testimony); the five-step syllogism; rules of debate
- **Vaisheshika** (attr. Kanada) — atomism; six categories of being *(the Democritus move, made independently)*
- **Purva Mimamsa** (attr. Jaimini) — the Veda is eternal and *authorless* (apaurusheya); ritual hermeneutics; deep philosophy of language and testimony
- **Vedanta / Uttara Mimamsa** (Badarayana's *Brahma Sutras*, c. 200 BCE–200 CE) — the Upanishads systematized into 555 aphorisms *(so terse that every later school fights its war as a commentary on them)*

---

## VI. THE AGE OF DIALECTIC (c. 100–700 CE)
*Buddhist, Jain, and Hindu philosophers argue for six centuries — and sharpen each other into greatness*

### Mahayana turns
- **Nagarjuna** (c. 150–250) — *Madhyamaka*: emptiness (*shunyata*) — nothing has intrinsic nature; two truths (conventional/ultimate); the tetralemma *(the most influential Indian philosopher after the Buddha; → Gaudapada, Zen, Tibet)*
- **Asanga & Vasubandhu** (4th c.) — *Yogachara*: consciousness-only; the three natures; Vasubandhu's *Abhidharmakosha* as the encyclopedia of Buddhist psychology
### The epistemological turn
- **Dignaga** (c. 480–540) — Buddhist logic; only perception and inference; *apoha* — meaning as exclusion
  - → **Dharmakirti** (c. 600–660) — the summit of Buddhist epistemology; *Pramanavarttika* *(for the next 500 years, every Indian philosopher of any school must answer him)*
### Consolidations
- **Buddhaghosa** (5th c.) — *Visuddhimagga*; Theravada systematized *(→ modern vipassana, mindfulness)*
- **Umasvati** (c. 2nd–5th c.) — *Tattvartha Sutra*; Jain doctrine in sutra form, accepted by all Jain sects
- **Kundakunda** (early centuries CE, contested) — Jain two-truths doctrine; the pure soul behind conventional description
### The grammarians
- **Bhartrihari** (c. 450–510) — *shabda-brahman*: language is the ultimate reality; *sphota* theory of meaning; no cognition without language *(the linguistic turn, 1,400 years early)*

---

## VII. VEDANTA & TANTRA (c. 600–1300)
*Commentary wars over the nature of Brahman — and consciousness as power in Kashmir*

### The Advaita line
- **Gaudapada** (c. 6th c.) — *Mandukya Karika*; *ajativada*: nothing is ever really born *(so close to Nagarjuna that rivals cried crypto-Buddhism)*
  - → **Shankara** (c. 700–750) — **Advaita**: only nirguna Brahman is real; the world is *maya* (appearance); liberation by knowledge alone; commentaries on the triple canon; four monasteries *(the great consolidator — the Kant of the lineage: everyone after responds)*
### Mimamsa's last stand
- **Kumarila Bhatta** (c. 700) — defended Vedic ritualism against Buddhist critique; intrinsic validity of cognition
- **Prabhakara** (c. 700) — rival Mimamsa school; knowledge never errs, error is incomplete grasp
- **Mandana Mishra** (c. 700) — sphota + Advaita; the legendary debate with Shankara, judged — tradition says — by Mandana's wife, Ubhaya Bharati
### The synthesizers & logicians
- **Vachaspati Mishra** (c. 900–980) — "master of all systems"; authoritative commentaries on all six darshanas
- **Udayana** (c. 1000) — Nyaya theism; *Nyayakusumanjali*: formal arguments for God's existence *(caps the 600-year Hindu–Buddhist debate as Buddhism fades from India)*
### Kashmir Shaivism — consciousness as power
- **Vasugupta** (c. 800–850) — *Shiva Sutras*; reality as the vibration (*spanda*) of one consciousness
- **Utpaladeva** (c. 900–950) — *pratyabhijna* (recognition): you do not become Shiva; you recognize you already are
- **Abhinavagupta** (c. 950–1016) — *Tantraloka*; and the *rasa* theory of aesthetics: art as a taste of liberation *(philosopher, mystic, and the greatest aesthetician of the tradition)*
### Vedanta contra Shankara
- **Ramanuja** (1017–1137) — *Vishishtadvaita* (qualified non-dualism): the world and souls are real, as God's body; bhakti as the means *(channels the Tamil Alvars into high Vedanta)*
- **Madhva** (1238–1317) — *Dvaita* (dualism): five eternal differences; difference is real, forever *(→ Chaitanya's Bengal)*

---

## VIII. THE BHAKTI CENTURIES (c. 600–1700)
*Philosophy leaves Sanskrit: devotion, dissent, and vernacular song*

### The southern spring
- **Alvars & Nayanars** (600–900) — Tamil hymns to Vishnu and Shiva; devotion as its own epistemology *(→ Ramanuja; the movement then travels north for a millennium)*
- **Basava** (12th c.) — Lingayat *vachanas*: "work is worship"; caste and temple rejected
### Vernacular Vedanta
- **Jnaneshwar** (1275–1296) — the Gita sung in Marathi at age ~16; Advaita for farmers
### The nirguna sants — the formless God
- **Kabir** (c. 1440–1518) — weaver-poet; neither Hindu nor Muslim; the formless God within; scripture and priesthood mocked in verse *(→ Nanak; → Tagore's favorite)*
- **Guru Nanak** (1469–1539) — one formless God; no caste; honest work and shared bread — a new path: Sikhism
### Saguna floods — God with form and story
- **Chaitanya** (1486–1534) — ecstatic Krishna-bhakti as metaphysics: *achintya bheda abheda* — inconceivable difference-and-nondifference *(→ Bengal Vaishnavism; → ISKCON)*
- **Mirabai** (c. 1498–1547) — Rajput princess; devotion as defiance of family, caste, and convention
- **Tulsidas** (1532–1623) — *Ramcharitmanas*: Rama's story for everyone, in Awadhi
- **Tukaram** (1608–1649) — *abhangas* to Vitthala; the everyman's direct line to God

---

## IX. NAVYA-NYAYA: THE NEW LOGIC (c. 1300–1700)
*A technical language for cognition — precision instruments, centuries before Frege*

- **Gangesha** (c. 1320) — *Tattvachintamani*; epistemology refounded with a new formal vocabulary
  - → **Raghunatha Shiromani** (c. 1477–1547) — the categories themselves rebuilt; metaphysics revised from inside logic
    - → **Gadadhara** (17th c.) — the technical apex *(Navya-Nyaya's analytic machinery is what Matilal and Ingalls later compared, seriously, to modern logic)*

---

## X. THE MODERN ENCOUNTER (1800–now)
*Colonial rupture: reform, revival, and a reply to the West*

### Reform
- **Rammohan Roy** (1772–1833) — Brahmo Samaj; Upanishads + Enlightenment reason; campaigns against sati *(the "father of the Indian renaissance")*
- **Dayananda Saraswati** (1824–1883) — Arya Samaj; "back to the Vedas"; ritualism and caste-by-birth rejected
### Revival — Vedanta answers back
- **Ramakrishna** (1836–1886) — tried every path, reported one summit: "as many faiths, so many paths"
  - → **Vivekananda** (1863–1902) — practical Vedanta; Chicago 1893: Hinduism speaks to the West in its own defense
- **Aurobindo** (1872–1950) — integral yoga; evolution continued into the divine; matter to supermind
### Poets & prophets
- **Tagore** (1861–1941) — the religion of man; universalism against nationalism *(debated Gandhi on the nation, Einstein on reality)*
- **Muhammad Iqbal** (1877–1938) — *khudi* (the self) and the reconstruction of religious thought in Islam; Nietzsche and Bergson read through Sufism
### Freedom's philosophers
- **Gandhi** (1869–1948) — *satyagraha*: truth-force; ahimsa scaled from Jain ethics to mass politics; "Truth is God" *(→ Martin Luther King, Mandela)*
- **Ambedkar** (1891–1956) — *Annihilation of Caste*; drafted the Constitution; led half a million into a rationalist Navayana Buddhism, 1956 *(the tradition's fiercest internal critic)*
### The silent teachers
- **Ramana Maharshi** (1879–1950) — self-enquiry: hold the single question "Who am I?"
- **J. Krishnamurti** (1895–1986) — "truth is a pathless land"; dissolved the messianic order built for him; choiceless awareness
### The academy — Indian philosophy as a discipline
- **K.C. Bhattacharyya** (1875–1949) — "Svaraj in Ideas"; the subject as freedom *(Kant answered from Calcutta)*
- **Radhakrishnan** (1888–1975) — interpreter between the two worlds; philosopher-president
- **B.K. Matilal** (1935–1991) — Indian logic and epistemology in the analytic idiom (Oxford chair); demolished the "mystical East" cliché
- **J.N. Mohanty** (1928–2023) — Husserl's phenomenology in dialogue with Nyaya
- **Daya Krishna** (1924–2007) — interrogated every received myth *about* Indian philosophy, including "spirituality" itself
- **Amartya Sen** (b. 1933) — the argumentative Indian; *nyaya* (realized justice) vs *niti* (rules); capabilities *(→ Nussbaum)*

---

## XI. THE ADJACENT GIANTS — grammar, number, medicine, art
*Not darshana by department; lineage-shapers by effect*

- **Pingala** (c. 3rd–2nd c. BCE) — prosody via binary patterns of long/short syllables *(binary representation, two millennia before Leibniz)*
- **Charaka & Sushruta** (early centuries CE) — medical compendia; Charaka embeds Samkhya-style physiology and a manual for structured debate
- **Bharata Muni** (c. 200 BCE–200 CE, attr.) — *Natyashastra*: drama, music, and the first theory of *rasa* *(→ Abhinavagupta)*
- **Aryabhata** (476–550) — the Earth rotates; sine tables; place-value astronomy
- **Brahmagupta** (598–668) — rules for **zero** as a number, and for negatives *(shunya the numeral; shunyata the concept — a rhyme, not a derivation, but the same civilization made both moves)*
- **Madhava of Sangamagrama** (c. 1340–1425) — Kerala school; infinite series for π and trigonometric functions *(calculus's core results, ~250 years before Newton and Leibniz)*

## THE SPINE (if you remember nothing else)

```
Vedas (ritual, cosmic order — and doubt)
   → Upanishads (the turn inward: Brahman = Atman, karma, moksha)
      → SHRAMANA REVOLT: Buddha / Mahavira / Charvaka
         (no-self · ahimsa · materialism — the astika/nastika fork)
         → Gita & epics (action reconciled with knowledge)
            → Six darshanas in sutra (Samkhya–Yoga, Nyaya–Vaisheshika, Mimamsa–Vedanta)
               → Age of dialectic: Nagarjuna → Dignaga/Dharmakirti vs Nyaya & Mimamsa
                  → SHANKARA (Advaita consolidation)
                     → Ramanuja / Madhva (theistic rejoinders)
                        → Bhakti centuries (vernacular devotion)  ·  Navya-Nyaya (new logic)
                           → COLONIAL ENCOUNTER: Roy (reform) → Vivekananda (revival)
                              → Gandhi / Ambedkar / the academy (Matilal, Sen)
```

**Four recurring pivots:**
1. **The Upanishadic turn** — from outer ritual to the knowing self
2. **Buddha & Mahavira** — Vedic authority refused; thought forks into āstika and nāstika
3. **Shankara** — the debate age consolidated under Vedanta
4. **The colonial encounter** — Indian thought reformed, revived, and replying to the West

**How this lineage differs from the Western map:**
- **Commentary, not treatise.** Progress runs sutra → bhashya → varttika → tika: philosophy as an argument conducted across centuries inside layered commentaries.
- **The shared problem is liberation.** Epistemology and logic are (mostly) instruments of moksha — though Navya-Nyaya eventually did logic for logic's sake.
- **Authorship dissolves.** "Vyasa," "Kapila," "Kanada," "Brihaspati" are attributions; the schools are the real authors. The idea outlives the name.
- **The losers are erased.** Charvaka and the Ajivikas survive only in their opponents' quotations — a whole materialist tradition known by hostile paraphrase.
- **Contact ran both ways.** Pyrrho traveled to India with Alexander and came home a skeptic; Schopenhauer read the Upanishads nightly; modern linguistics acknowledges Panini as the first generative grammar.
